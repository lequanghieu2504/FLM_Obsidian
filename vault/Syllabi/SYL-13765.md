## Description
This course provides foundational knowledge of classical electrodynamics for students in electrical engineering, electronics, and telecommunications. It introduces the essential laws and principles governing electromagnetic fields, including Maxwell’s equations, the behavior of electric and magnetic fields, and methods for analyzing electromagnetic phenomena in various media. The specialization emphasizes mathematical rigor, physical intuition, and problem-solving skills. Students will explore topics such as radiation, waveguides, antennas, electromagnetic energy flow, and field interactions with matter. Through conceptual explanations and applied exercises, learners develop the ability to analyze, model, and interpret real-world electromagnetic phenomena used in modern technologies including communication systems, high-frequency circuits, and advanced materials.

| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | CLO1 | Understanding fundamental principles of electromagnetism supported by essential mathematical tools to analyze and visualize electrostatic phenomena |
| 2 | CLO2 | Apply symmetry-based and analytical methods to calculate electric fields, and explain the concepts of susceptibility, polarization, dielectrics, and dipole formation in electromagnetic systems. |
| 3 | CLO3 | Apply concepts and solutions from electric field analysis to magnetostatics, interpret Maxwell’s equations within this context, and explain how electromotive force, magnetic energy, and introductory quantum-mechanical principles relate to practical electromagnetic systems and device operation. |
| 4 | CLO4 | Use Maxwell’s equations to derive and analyze electromagnetic wave behavior, apply relativistic corrections to moving-charge problems, and understand how AC circuit components influence circuit operation. |
