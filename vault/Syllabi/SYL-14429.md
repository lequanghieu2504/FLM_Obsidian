## Description
The IT Interdisciplinary Graduation Project is an integrative capstone experience that consolidates the knowledge, skills, and competencies acquired throughout the undergraduate into a practical, research-oriented project. This course challenges students from the disciplines of IT to collaboratively apply their analytical, problem-solving, and technical expertise to address real-world problems. By working across disciplinary boundaries, student teams are expected to design, implement, and evaluate solutions that reflect the convergence of modern IT fields. This course is structured for teams of 3–6 members, drawn from any combination of the IT specializations (IT, IA, SE, AI,...), thereby fostering interdisciplinary collaboration and teamwork. Individual enrollment is permitted under specific conditions approved by the supervising lecturer and head of department/faculty. All projects must incorporate a substantive research component, regardless of specialization. This includes: - Problem Definition: A clear articulation of the research problem and its significance within the IT domain - Literature Review: A critical synthesis of relevant prior work, identifying gaps that the project aims to address - Proposed Solution or Model: A well-justified technical approach (e.g., an AI/ML model, a security mechanism, a software system, or a hybrid combination thereof) - Experimental Evaluation: Rigorous testing and empirical analysis to validate the proposed solution, with quantitative and/or qualitative results - Discussion, Conclusion, and Future Work: Interpretation of findings and directions for further research

Before thesis defense, the student team must wait until the second round of thesis defense if the whole team has the OGA less than 5/10 OR they are in one of below cases: Case 1 – Incomplete Research Components The team fails to complete essential research components, including problem definition, literature review, methodology, or experimental evaluation. Case 2 – Insufficient Literature Review The literature review lacks sufficient academic sources (e.g., fewer than the required number of peer-reviewed papers) or fails to provide critical analysis and comparison. Case 3 – Lack of Research Contribution The project does not demonstrate a clear research contribution (e.g., no proposed model, algorithm, framework, or meaningful improvement over existing solutions). Case 4 – Missing or Invalid Experimental Results The team fails to conduct experiments, or the results are invalid due to improper methodology, lack of dataset, or absence of evaluation metrics. Case 5 – Poor Implementation Quality The developed system is incomplete, non-functional, or does not reflect the proposed research solution. Case 6 – Weak Analysis and Discussion The team fails to analyze results critically, lacks justification of findings, or does not discuss limitations and implications. Case 7 – Failure to Submit Required Documents Case 8 – Lack of Traceability Between Research and System There is no clear linkage between the proposed research contribution and the implemented system. Case 9 – Academic Integrity Violation There is considered to occur when similarity exceeds 30%, including any instances of plagiarism, falsification of data, or the use of external sources without appropriate citation. Case 10 – Inadequate Presentation Readiness The team is unable to clearly present and defend their work, including explaining the research problem, methodology, and results. The student(s) has to re-do the capstone project if the above cases occur with the assessments for the second thesis defense OR there is one OGA with grade less than 2/10. After thesis defense, one or more team members are evaluated as failed the capstone project if they are in one of below cases (no matter when the guiding teacher doesn't evaluate that): (1) Student can't demonstrate any function or use case (2) Students can't prove the fact that they prepare any of the project reports or the output software by themselves |
